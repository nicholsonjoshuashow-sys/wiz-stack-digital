Incident Response Triage & Scoping Workflow

A repeatable path from first alert to a scoped engagement. Work top-to-bottom; capture everything in a timestamped case log. The goal of triage is not to fix — it is to confirm an incident, contain blast radius, preserve evidence, and scope the work.

The 6-phase flow (PICERL, aligned to NIST SP 800-61r2 & SANS)

1 · PREPARE

Tooling ready (Velociraptor, KAPE/CAPE, SIFT), comms plan, authority to act, evidence handling / chain-of-custody template.

2 · IDENTIFY

Validate the alert. Real incident or false positive? Assign severity. Establish the initial timeline & scope.

3 · CONTAIN

Short-term (isolate host, disable account, block C2) then long-term. Preserve volatile evidence before pulling the plug.

4 · ERADICATE

Remove persistence, malware, rogue accounts, forwarding rules. Identify & close the initial access vector.

5 · RECOVER

Restore from known-good, rebuild, re-enable, monitor for reinfection. Validate before returning to production.

6 · LESSONS

Blameless postmortem, IOCs to detections, report to client/regulators, update runbooks.

First 60 minutes — order of operations

  1. Confirm & log. Who reported, when, what was seen. Start the case clock (UTC + local).
  2. Establish authority & scope of consent. What are you authorized to touch? (esp. personal devices / classified environments — use screen-share, avoid pulling sensitive data offsite.)
  3. Do no harm to evidence. Prefer live-response collection over reboot/reimage. Order of volatility: RAM → network state → running procs → disk → logs → archival.
  4. Isolate, don't power off (unless ransomware actively encrypting). Network-isolate to preserve memory.
  5. Triage collection. Run the host commands (Tab ②) or deploy Velociraptor/KAPE (Tab ③) for the OS in scope.
  6. Identify accounts/tokens at risk. Reset creds, revoke sessions/tokens, kill OAuth grants & mail forwarding rules.
  7. Assign severity (table below) and communicate status to stakeholders.
  8. Scope: how many hosts/identities/tenants? Data touched? Regulatory triggers (HIPAA/PCI/PII/breach law)?

Severity matrix

SevDefinitionResponse
SEV-1Active attacker, mass encryption, confirmed data exfil, domain/tenant compromise, safety impact.All-hands, war room, exec + legal notify immediately.
SEV-2Single-host/identity compromise with lateral movement potential; BEC with fraud in flight.Dedicated IC, contain within hours, hourly updates.
SEV-3Contained single account (phish), no spread, no confirmed data loss.Standard triage, same-day.
SEV-4Suspicious activity, unconfirmed; policy/hygiene issue.Investigate, monitor, document.
Scoping questions never skip: initial access vector · dwell time · persistence · privilege level reached · data accessed vs exfiltrated · backups intact? · is the actor still in?

Evidence-handling reminders

  • Hash every artifact on acquisition (sha256) and record it in the chain-of-custody log.
  • Never work on originals — image, verify hash, work on copies. Use write-blockers for physical media.
  • Record collector name, tool + version, timestamps, and system clock offset for every acquisition.
  • For cloud/M365: pull the Unified Audit Log & mailbox audit immediately — retention windows expire.
  • Screen-share when client data is sensitive/classified; document what you observed rather than exfiltrating it.

PowerShell — running processes with paths & hashes

Persistence — autoruns, services, tasks, WMI

PowerShell — the usual persistence homes
# Run/RunOnce keys (HKLM + HKCU)
Get-ItemProperty HKLM:\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run*,
  HKCU:\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run*
Get-CimInstance Win32_StartupCommand | Select Name,Command,Location,User
# Services with non-standard / user-writable paths
Get-CimInstance Win32_Service | Where {$_.PathName -notmatch 'C:\\Windows'} |
  Select Name,State,StartMode,PathName
# Scheduled tasks
Get-ScheduledTask | Where State -ne 'Disabled' |
  Select TaskName,TaskPath,@{n='Action';e={($_.Actions.Execute)}}
schtasks /query /fo LIST /v
# WMI event subscriptions (fileless persistence)
Get-WmiObject -Namespace root\Subscription -Class __EventFilter
Get-WmiObject -Namespace root\Subscription -Class CommandLineEventConsumer
Gold standard: Sysinternals Autoruns — autorunsc.exe -accepteula -a * -c -h -s * > autoruns.csv (VirusTotal-enrich with -vt).

Logon / replication / DCSync hunting
# DC security events: 4624/4672 (priv logon), 4768/4769 (Kerberos TGT/TGS),

# DCSync detection = 4662 with replication GUID from a non-DC principal
Get-WinEvent -FilterHashtable @{LogName='Security';Id=4662} |

Do not reboot a DC to "clear" anything until memory + AD DB (ntds.dit) and SYSVOL are preserved. Consider golden/silver ticket & DCSync exposure → plan a double krbtgt reset during recovery.
BEC = Business Email Compromise. Time is money — fraudulent wires may be in flight. Freeze the fraud, preserve logs, then investigate. Retention windows on audit logs are short: pull them now.

BEC triage walk-through (Microsoft 365 / Exchange Online)

  1. Contain the identity. Reset password, revoke all sessions/tokens, require MFA re-registration, disable legacy auth.
  2. Kill the actor's persistence: mailbox rules (auto-forward/delete), mail-flow (transport) rules, delegated/OAuth app grants, added mobile devices, forwarding SMTP addresses.
  3. Preserve: Unified Audit Log, mailbox audit, message trace, Azure AD sign-in logs. Export before anything expires.
  4. Scope: which mailboxes, what was read/exported, any rogue global admins, spoofed look-alike domains, fraudulent invoices sent.
  5. Notify: finance/AP to halt wires; bank recall; affected external parties; legal/regulatory.

Connect & hunt malicious inbox / transport rules

PowerShell — Exchange Online + Graph
Connect-ExchangeOnline -UserPrincipalName admin@contoso.com
Connect-IPPSSession        # Security & Compliance for audit search
# Malicious inbox rules across ALL mailboxes (forward/delete/move to RSS)
Get-Mailbox -ResultSize Unlimited | ForEach-Object {
  Get-InboxRule -Mailbox $_.UserPrincipalName |
  Where {$_.ForwardTo -or $_.RedirectTo -or $_.ForwardAsAttachmentTo -or $_.DeleteMessage} |
  Select @{n='Mbx';e={$_.MailboxOwnerId}},Name,Enabled,ForwardTo,RedirectTo,DeleteMessage }
# Tenant-wide / mailbox forwarding addresses
Get-Mailbox -ResultSize Unlimited |
  Where {$_.ForwardingSmtpAddress -or $_.ForwardingAddress} |
  Select DisplayName,ForwardingSmtpAddress,DeliverToMailboxAndForward
# Malicious mail-flow (transport) rules
Get-TransportRule | Select Name,State,BlindCopyTo,RedirectMessageTo,Description

Audit logs, sign-ins & message trace

PowerShell — Unified Audit Log & suspicious ops
# Everything a compromised user did in the last 7 days
Search-UnifiedAuditLog -StartDate (Get-Date).AddDays(-7) -EndDate (Get-Date) `
  -UserIds victim@contoso.com -ResultSize 5000 | Export-Csv ual.csv -NoTypeInformation
# High-signal operations to filter for:
#  New-InboxRule, Set-InboxRule, UpdateInboxRules, Add-MailboxPermission,
#  Set-Mailbox (ForwardingSmtpAddress), New-TransportRule, Consent to application,
#  Add service principal, Update user (MFA/auth methods), MailItemsAccessed
Search-UnifiedAuditLog -StartDate (Get-Date).AddDays(-30) -EndDate (Get-Date) `
  -Operations "New-InboxRule,Set-InboxRule,Add-MailboxPermission,MailItemsAccessed" -ResultSize 5000
Message trace + rogue OAuth apps (Graph)
# Did the attacker blast invoices/phish? Trace outbound
Get-MessageTraceV2 -SenderAddress victim@contoso.com -StartDate (Get-Date).AddDays(-10) -EndDate (Get-Date)
# Rogue app consents / service principals (illicit OAuth grant)
Connect-MgGraph -Scopes "Directory.Read.All","AuditLog.Read.All"
Get-MgOauth2PermissionGrant -All | Select ClientId,ConsentType,Scope
Get-MgAuditLogSignIn -Top 200 -Filter "userPrincipalName eq 'victim@contoso.com'" |
  Select CreatedDateTime,IpAddress,AppDisplayName,
  @{n='Loc';e={$_.Location.CountryOrRegion}},ConflictWithLocation,Status
Look for: impossible-travel sign-ins, sign-ins from VPS/hosting ASNs, new "mail-reading" OAuth apps, rules that hide replies (move to RSS/Archive + mark read), and look-alike domains (one letter off — e.g. contos0.com).

Processes, network & C2

bash
ps auxfww                                 # full process tree
ss -tunap                                 # sockets + owning PID/program
lsof -i -Pn | grep -i estab              # established connections
# deleted-but-running binaries (classic malware tell)
ls -la /proc/*/exe 2>/dev/null | grep -i deleted

Persistence — cron, services, rc, profiles, modules

bash
for u in $(cut -f1 -d: /etc/passwd); do crontab -l -u $u 2>/dev/null; done
cat /etc/crontab; ls -la /etc/cron.*/ /var/spool/cron/
systemctl list-unit-files --state=enabled; systemctl list-timers --all

# shell init files often carry backdoors

# LD_PRELOAD rootkit & loaded kernel modules
cat /etc/ld.so.preload 2>/dev/null; lsmod; kmod list

Threat Intelligence Dashboard

Where to enrich an IOC and what each platform is best at. Match the artifact type (hash / IP / domain / URL / email) to the right tool, and the situation to the right source.

VirusTotal ↗

Files, hashes, IPs, domains, URLs. 70+ AV engines, behavioral sandboxes, relations graph, community comments. The default first-stop for any IOC.

Use when: you have a hash/URL and need reputation + pivot pfast. VT Graph to map related infrastructure. Mind OPSEC — uploads are shared.

LevelBlue OTX (AlienVault) ↗

Community feed / "Pulses". 19M+ indicators daily, campaign context bundling IOCs with narrative. Free API.

Use when: you want to know if an IOC is tied to a named campaign/actor and grab the full indicator set for detections.

AbuseIPDB ↗

IP reputation. Crowd-sourced abuse reports with confidence score & categories (brute-force, C2, scanning).

Use when: triaging a suspicious source IP from logs/sign-ins — is it a known-bad / abusive host?

GreyNoise ↗

Internet-noise context. Tells you if an IP is mass-scanning the whole internet (benign noise) vs targeted.

Use when: you need to de-prioritize alert noise or confirm an IP is opportunistic scanning vs specifically after you.

Pulsedive ↗

IOC enrichment + risk scoring for IPs/domains/URLs with passive DNS, ports, and linked threats. Generous free tier.

Use when: you want a single enriched risk view of a domain/IP with related indicators and no login friction.

ThreatFox / URLhaus (abuse.ch) ↗

Fresh malware C2 & payload URLs. Free, high-fidelity, exportable feeds (also MalwareBazaar for samples, Feodo Tracker for botnet C2).

Use when: you extracted a C2 from CAPE and want corroboration, or need a blocklist of live malware infrastructure.

IBM X-Force Exchange ↗

Aggregated intel + reports. IP/URL/malware scoring, vulnerability data, curated threat reports. SIEM integration.

Use when: you want vendor-grade context/scoring and written threat reports to support a client briefing.

Shodan ↗

Internet-exposed asset intel. What services/banners/vulns an IP exposes; historical scans; SSL/JARM pivoting.

Use when: profiling attacker infra (what's the C2 host running?) or checking a victim's external exposure.

Censys ↗

Attack-surface & certificate intel. Deep host/cert search; pivot on TLS certs and JARM to find related attacker infrastructure.

Use when: mapping an actor's broader infrastructure from one seed IP/cert/domain.

MISP ↗

Threat-sharing platform. Store, correlate, and share IOCs/events across trust groups; export to IDS/SIEM formats.

Use when: running your own intel repository across engagements or consuming ISAC/community feeds at scale.

ANY.RUN ↗

Interactive malware sandbox. Detonate & interact with a sample live in-browser; watch process tree, network, drops.

Use when: you need dynamic behavior + extracted IOCs from a suspicious file/URL and want to click through the detonation.

Hybrid Analysis ↗

Free Falcon Sandbox. Static + dynamic file analysis with MITRE ATT&CK mapping and downloadable IOCs.

Use when: you want a free, thorough automated malware report with ATT&CK technique mapping.

MITRE ATT&CK ↗

TTP knowledge base. Map observed behavior to techniques, pivot to actor groups, and drive detection/hunt coverage.

Use when: framing what the attacker did, communicating it to stakeholders, and finding gaps in your hunt.

CISA KEV Catalog ↗

Known Exploited Vulnerabilities. Authoritative list of CVEs actively exploited in the wild, with due-date guidance.

Use when: determining if the likely initial-access vector is a known, actively-exploited vuln to prioritize patching.

OSINT & IOC Repository Search

Once you have IOCs from the host or CAPE, pivot through OSINT sources and repositories to find related samples, infrastructure, credentials, and public exposure. Watch OPSEC — some lookups (esp. uploading samples) tip off the adversary.

MalwareBazaar ↗

Search & download malware samples by hash, YARA, tag, or imphash. Pivot from one sample to a family.

Use when: you have a hash and want the sample + related variants for analysis.

Maltego ↗ / SpiderFoot

Graph-based OSINT automation. Feed a domain/IP/email/name and auto-pivot across 200+ data sources into a link graph.

Use when: mapping relationships between IOCs, infrastructure, and identities during scoping.

GreyNoise Visualizer ↗ / OTX

Search indicators across community intel and scanning telemetry to attribute an IOC to campaigns.

Use when: enriching an IP/domain with actor/campaign attribution.

Have I Been Pwned ↗

Check whether an email/domain appears in known breaches — a likely credential-stuffing / initial-access source.

Use when: scoping how a victim account's credentials may have leaked; check domain search for org-wide exposure.

DeHashed / IntelX ↗

Search leaked credential datasets and paste/breach repositories by email, username, domain, IP, or password.

Use when: confirming which specific creds leaked and whether they match the compromised account.

GitHub / Gist code search ↗

Search public repos/gists for leaked secrets, hard-coded IOCs, and reused attacker tooling. Pair with trufflehog / gitleaks.

Use when: hunting for leaked org secrets, or finding attacker tooling/config posted publicly by IOC string.

urlscan.io ↗

The questionnaire — 9 domains

Expand each. These are the same questions sent to Granola for coverage analysis.

1 · Incident identification & reporting
  • Who first detected/reported it, and how (alert, user, third party)?
  • Date/time first observed vs first reported? What is the current status (ongoing vs contained)?
  • What exactly was observed (symptoms, anomalies)?
  • Has anyone already taken action that could affect evidence?
2 · Scope & affected assets
  • Which hosts, identities, mailboxes, tenants, cloud accounts, and networks are involved?
  • How many endpoints/users total in the environment (managed vs unmanaged)?
  • What business systems/data are affected (M365, AD, finance/ERP, SaaS)?
  • Is this a single-host issue or is there evidence of spread / lateral movement?
3 · Initial access & attack vector
  • Suspected entry point (phish, exposed service, stolen creds, vendor/supply chain, physical)?
  • Any known exploited vuln (check CISA KEV)? Unpatched internet-facing systems?
  • Were credentials leaked/reused (HIBP/DeHashed)? Was MFA present & enforced?
  • Timeline of first malicious activity vs first legitimate-looking anomaly?
4 · Attacker actions, persistence & privilege
  • What privilege level did the actor reach (user, local admin, domain/global admin)?
  • Persistence observed (rules, tasks, services, WMI, keys, OAuth grants)?
  • Rogue/new accounts, added devices, or forwarding rules?
  • Malware/C2 identified? Any extracted config/IOCs (IPs, domains, hashes)?
5 · Data impact & exfiltration
  • What data was accessed vs actually exfiltrated? Evidence for each?
  • Any regulated data (PII, PHI/HIPAA, PCI, classified/CUI)?
  • Financial impact — fraudulent wires/invoices in flight? Amounts & accounts?
  • Are backups intact, and are they clean / offline?
6 · Containment & eradication status
  • What containment is already done (isolation, disabled accounts, revoked sessions/tokens)?
  • Passwords reset? MFA enforced? Forwarding/transport rules removed?
  • Is the actor believed to still have access? Any re-entry after containment?
  • Initial access vector closed?
7 · Evidence, tooling & forensics
  • What evidence is preserved (RAM, disk images, logs, audit exports) and where?
  • Chain of custody & hashing in place? Log retention windows at risk?
  • Access constraints (sensitive/classified data → screen-share only)?
  • What collection tooling is deployed (Velociraptor, KAPE, CAPE, cloud audit)?
8 · Stakeholders, authority & comms
  • Who is the incident commander / decision-maker? Authority to act on which systems?
  • Legal, insurance/cyber policy, and law-enforcement engaged?
  • Notification obligations (regulators, customers, partners) & deadlines?
  • Communication cadence & out-of-band channel (in case email is compromised)?
9 · Engagement scoping & next steps
  • Objective — contain & monitor, full forensics, root-cause, or attribution?
  • Estimated hours / effort & resource needs? Environment access method?
  • Deliverables (report, IOC list, remediation plan, breach notification support)?
  • Immediate next actions & owners with deadlines?
